# Abstract

Small open language models answer many factual questions wrongly and say so with the same confidence as when they are right. Problem statement 13 asks for probes on internal activations that flag likely errors, so that the model can abstain or retrieve, giving fewer wrong answers while most questions are still answered. KnowWhen-R does this for two frozen Cohere Labs models, Tiny Aya Global and Aya Expanse 8B.

We trained linear probes on hidden states with 9,954 questions from NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA and PopQA, labelled by whether each model's own answer was right, with and without reranked Wikipedia passages. Three probes work together: one judges the closed book answer, one forecasts whether a retrieved answer would be right, and one judges the retrieved answer. A question goes to the likelier path, and the least confident answers are withheld.

On 3,968 held out test questions, answering everything from memory gave 79.2% wrong answers on Tiny Aya and 65.8% on Aya Expanse. At exactly 70% of questions answered, routing left 29.0% and 23.1% of all questions wrong, against 49.7% and 37.1% for a closed book probe that abstains. Against that baseline the drop was 21 and 14 points, confirmed with Holm correction, and at equal coverage it stayed between 6 and 31 points on all four datasets when each was removed from training. After retrieval, activation probes reached 0.868 and 0.877 macro AUROC, against 0.782 and 0.724 for a logistic model on token statistics. A probe reading the prompt alone beat P(Yes) self rating on every dataset, and answer probes beat agreement between five samples in domain without generating extra samples.

The results also have clear limits. Most of the gain comes from retrieval plus the post retrieval probe. Routing adds only 1 to 3 points over always retrieving in domain and almost nothing on unseen datasets, though it makes 15 to 28% fewer retrieval calls. For closed book answers on unseen datasets, token statistics often match activations. On Tiny Aya, better passages removed more wrong answers than routing did. A coverage target set on other datasets drifts on an unseen one, from 56% to 83% at a 70% target. A label free fix, setting the cut from the last 100 questions, keeps routed coverage between 68.9% and 70.4% on every unseen dataset, with wrong answer rates close to a perfectly matched cut. HotpotQA remains weak. On 600 MKQA questions in five other languages, the English trained probe stayed within 0.035 AUROC of English with no retraining. So activation probes are most useful as a check on retrieved answers, and the answer cut should follow the incoming questions.

The pipeline chains CuratorKIT for deduplication, AuditKIT for an independent replay of decisions, and SafeTune's probe guard for live routing. CircuitKIT was attempted and did not fit in GPU memory. All code, probes, traces and failed runs are public.
